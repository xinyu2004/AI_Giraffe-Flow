In-vehicle network

Past: Controllers exchanged a fixed signal list on CAN, and diagnostics used that same bus. LIN connected simple body parts such as switches and lamps. FlexRay gave the chassis a stricter synchronization. MOST carried infotainment. Those buses were too narrow for cameras and large software payloads.

Present: On new-energy vehicles the backbone is Ethernet, and central compute with zonal controllers is already in production. Services use SOME/IP. Diagnostics and flashing use DoIP. DDS distributes data: nodes publish and subscribe by topic, with timing and reliability written in QoS. Time-sensitive flows such as cameras are sent by TSN on the gPTP clock. Actuators and simple nodes still use CAN FD.

Later: The backbone needs a higher rate, and simple edge nodes may move to 10BASE-T1S. Zenoh aims to carry vehicle data to the edge and the cloud with one publish, subscribe, and query protocol. It is not an in-vehicle standard yet.

Ciphers and keys are in AI Fortress Auto.
